## Consolidation, repacking, and parcel preview are different services

Parcel consolidation means combining eligible warehouse purchases into one outgoing shipment. Repacking changes how those goods are packaged, such as removing unnecessary outer cartons or adding protection. A parcel preview or rehearsal involves packing and measuring goods before the next shipping decision; the precise scope and approval sequence depend on the provider.

Combining orders does not necessarily mean removing every seller box, compressing the contents, or checking every product in detail. A warehouse photo of an item also does not establish how the finished parcel will be packed.

> A useful consolidation service gives the buyer control over which goods ship together, what packaging is retained, and how the finished parcel is measured. The decision should use the packed parcel and an eligible shipping line, rather than the sum of product weights or an assumption that one box is always cheaper.

**Confirm the output of a packing service.** Ask whether you receive weight, outer dimensions, packaging information, a revised quote, or an opportunity to approve changes. A service name alone does not establish that all of these are included.

## What actually determines whether consolidation saves money?

A combined shipment is financially preferable only when its complete cost is lower than the acceptable separate-shipment alternative. Count applicable freight, packing, preview, handling, storage, and arrival charges, using consistent assumptions for both options. Costs already included in a quote should not be added again.

Chargeable weight is the weight used to price a shipment. Depending on the line, it may reflect actual packed weight, volumetric weight calculated from outer dimensions, or other minimum and rounding rules. The applicable divisor and formula must come from that line.

Removing empty space may improve a parcel’s dimensions. Adding a protective outer carton may increase them. A combined parcel can also become ineligible for a line that accepted the individual items because of its size, weight, or contents.

**Compare finished packing alternatives, not just item weights.** Ask for a practical combined plan and, where useful, a plan that separates the bulky, restricted, or fragile item. A non-numeric comparison can identify the right plan to quote without inventing a saving.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Several soft clothing purchases | Remove unnecessary outer packaging; consider suitable compression | One purchase is delayed or needs a return decision | Packed dimensions, service charges, storage and seller deadlines |
| Shoes with retail boxes you want to keep | Retain the requested boxes with an appropriate outer package | Box dimensions make a shared parcel costly or unsuitable | Measurements and quotes for the packing options you accept |
| Clothing plus a battery-containing item | Combine only if a line accepts the complete contents | The battery item restricts otherwise eligible routes | Written product acceptance and packing requirements |

## Storage and return deadlines can outweigh another arrival

Waiting for a final seller can affect goods that arrived earlier. Warehouse storage and seller-return windows are different deadlines and may begin at different events. Check each item’s dates instead of treating the whole basket as if it arrived together.

A purchase awaiting clarification should be dealt with while the applicable return process remains available. Removing tags, discarding packaging, or opening certain goods can affect eligibility. An agent may assist with a request, but seller acceptance is not guaranteed.

**Set a decision date for delayed purchases.** Compare the likely benefit of waiting with the applicable storage costs, return conditions, and your deadline. Ask whether the ready goods can leave separately if the last order is delayed further; do not assume unlimited storage or free extensions.

## Ask all four agents for the same consolidation plan

A consolidation enquiry should identify the proposed contents and how the parcel should be handled. Before purchasing, provide links and variants; for goods already stored, include warehouse order identifiers and the arrival status of each item.

- State the country and postcode, product types, quantities, and any batteries, liquids, fragile materials, or unusual dimensions.

- Specify packaging to retain, changes you would accept, and purchases to exclude.

- Ask about a preview or rehearsal if required: its charge, output, approval stage, and any further repacking fee.

- Request storage deadlines, final weight and dimensions, eligible lines, freight adjustments, and quote exclusions.

- Ask which arrival charges remain payable and how insurance applies to the proposed contents and packaging.

The same brief makes replies comparable and reveals when a provider is quoting a different level of protection or service. If the basket includes orders you bought yourself, ask whether they can join agent-purchased goods under the same account and terms; do not assume every provider permits that combination.

If CFANS fits the required process, send the brief to support@cfans.com to confirm the consolidation, preview, packing, and destination arrangements before committing to the purchases or parcel.

## Questions about choosing a consolidation agent

### Which Taobao agent is cheapest for consolidation?

The cheapest suitable option depends on the same basket, destination, acceptable packaging, and complete current quote. A zero basic consolidation fee can coexist with charges for preview, repacking, materials, storage, or freight. Compare what you will actually pay to send and receive the goods.

### Can I move purchases from my current agent to another consolidator?

Confirm both sides before paying. The current warehouse must permit domestic dispatch, and the receiving provider must accept and identify the goods. Compare release procedures, Chinese domestic freight, handling, and deadlines; another agent’s warehouse address alone is insufficient.

### Can several buyers share one consolidated parcel?

Ask the provider whether that arrangement is allowed and how ownership, recipient details, payments, and after-sales claims are handled. Do not assume an account designed for one buyer supports shared orders or that splitting the contents after arrival is part of the service.

### Does consolidation guarantee a single delivery date?

No. Packing several purchases into one shipment does not guarantee when it arrives. Seller dispatch, warehouse work, line availability, customs, and final delivery affect the schedule. Clarify whether an urgent item should wait for the rest.

### Does insurance cover every item inside a consolidated parcel?

Only the actual policy terms establish coverage. Check eligible contents, covered events, limits, exclusions, required evidence, and claim windows. Listing an item in a parcel does not by itself prove that every loss or damage scenario is covered.

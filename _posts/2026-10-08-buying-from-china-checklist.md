Before buying from China, confirm the exact product, the seller's conditions, the inspection you need, and whether the item can travel on a suitable route. Then compare the full delivered cost. The product price alone cannot answer whether an order makes sense.

I split the decision into three checkpoints: before purchasing, while the goods are in China, and before international shipment.

## Before purchasing: define the order

Save the product link and record the model, colour, size, quantity, and selected options. For clothing, compare the seller's measurements with a garment that fits you. A size label alone is a weak basis for choosing a fit.

For a multi-unit order, ask whether the minimum quantity applies to each variant or to the whole order. Confirm what happens if a colour or size is unavailable. Do not assume that a listing price covers every variation.

Keep the seller's dispatch conditions with your notes. Stock items, pre-orders, and made-to-order products need different planning. If a deadline matters, assess the complete chain rather than only the international transit estimate.

### Direct purchase or shopping agent?

Check whether the marketplace's own payment and delivery options serve your location and item. An agent may help when you need purchasing coordination, warehouse photos, or the ability to group orders from different sellers.

Compare providers using the same destination, product category, inspection scope, payment method, storage needs, and return requirements. A single service-fee percentage is not a complete comparison. Ask for the conditions that apply to your particular order.

## At the warehouse: review what can still be changed

Ask which visible details are checked by default and whether measurements or close-up images require an additional service. A useful photo request names the size label, seam, model number, or dimension you want to see. “Check the quality” leaves too much room for different interpretations.

Routine warehouse photos do not establish brand authenticity, electrical function, durability, or personal fit. If a specialist test matters, confirm that it is actually offered before buying.

If something looks wrong, keep the images and ask about the applicable return process before authorising shipment. Seller acceptance, application windows, packaging, item condition, and domestic return costs may affect the outcome.

## Before shipping: compare the parcel, not just the rate

Ask for packed weight and dimensions, then check the route's chargeable-weight rules and applicable fees. When a quote is provisional, record what could change after final packing.

Consolidation may reduce duplicate packaging, but does not automatically produce the lowest total cost. Compare combined and separate parcel options where useful, considering item compatibility and route restrictions.

Removing boxes or compressing soft goods is a trade-off. Consider protection, presentation, and possible return conditions before approving changes. For fragile goods, a smaller parcel is not the only objective.

### Build a complete cost worksheet

Record these items where applicable:

- Product price and China domestic delivery.
- Purchasing, payment, currency conversion, and requested inspection charges.
- Packaging, storage, or selected services.
- International transport and route-specific surcharges.
- Destination import charges applying to the shipment.

Confirm whether each charge applies. Keep estimates separate from settled amounts, and compare quotes in the same currency using a consistent basis.

## Keep a record through delivery

Save the specification choices, order confirmation, warehouse photos, packing decisions, and shipment reference. Track the stages separately: purchase processing, seller dispatch, domestic movement, warehouse handling, international movement, and destination delivery.

When asking about a delay, name the stage and last recorded event. That produces a more useful question than asking only why the whole order is taking so long.

## Quick answers

### Does paying for the product include international shipping?

Confirm what the first payment covers. In a warehouse-based purchasing workflow, the product purchase and international parcel are commonly settled at separate stages.

### Can warehouse photos guarantee that an item is correct?

They help you review visible details within the agreed inspection scope. They are not a guarantee of fit, function, or authenticity.

### Should I always combine orders?

Compare actual parcel options first. The result depends on size, weight, packaging, item restrictions, and available routes.

## Before you confirm

Proceed when you can explain what you are buying, what has been checked, what the quote covers, and which uncertainties remain. Ask specific questions while the decision can still be changed.
